Who Lives Outside Melbourne's 15-Minute Library Zone?

This project explores public library accessibility across Greater Melbourne. Public libraries are important cultural and learning spaces, but access to them is not evenly distributed. This visualisation investigates which Greater Melbourne communities have nearby library services, which areas rely more heavily on transport, and where low access overlaps with socioeconomic disadvantage.

1. The Disadvantage Backdrop

Scroll to zoom · Drag to pan · Double-click to reset

Disadvantage is concentrated on Melbourne's outer fringe. Western and south-eastern growth corridors record the lowest IRSD scores, while inner-city and eastern suburbs show markedly higher advantage. This spatial divide shapes nearly every other dimension of access explored below.

2. Who Is Disadvantaged?

Low income and low education reinforce each other. SA2s in the lower-left quadrant combine below-median weekly incomes with lower degree-attainment rates. The darkest circles - IRSD deciles 1-3 - cluster almost entirely in this zone, confirming that educational and economic disadvantage overlap strongly across Melbourne's fringe suburbs.

Disadvantage Is About More Than Money

Low income and low educational attainment shape not just what people can afford, but how they move through their city. Before examining library infrastructure, it is worth asking: how do residents in disadvantaged suburbs get around, and what happens when they cannot drive?

3. How Do People Get Around?

Scroll to zoom · Drag to pan · Double-click to reset

Outer suburbs average nearly three cars per household. High car ownership in fringe growth areas reflects poor public transport coverage rather than affluence. Residents in these zones who cannot drive - children, the elderly, people with disabilities - depend entirely on walkable local services. When those services are absent, the consequences are severe.

4. Library Infrastructure

Scroll to zoom · Drag to pan · Double-click to reset

Library infrastructure is clustered. A handful of inner-city SA2s host multiple branches, while most outer suburbs have none. The concentration in inner Melbourne is immediately clear - outer growth corridors to the west and south-east show almost no presence. For many residents in these areas, no public library exists within a practical 15-minute walking distance.

5. Access per Resident

Scroll to zoom · Drag to pan · Double-click to reset

Outer western suburbs show the lowest library access per resident. Libraries per 10,000 residents reveals deep spatial inequality - inner and established suburbs sit at the dark end of the scale; fringe growth areas sit at near-zero.

Infrastructure Gaps Hit the Most Vulnerable Hardest

Libraries are clustered, and access is unequal. But the true harm only emerges when these infrastructure gaps are mapped against the communities least able to seek alternatives. The following charts bring disadvantage and library access into direct conversation.

6. Disadvantage Meets Low Access

The most disadvantaged SA2s are also the least served by libraries. Each circle is one SA2, sized by population. Because many SA2s have no public library branch, the access values are scaled to make near-zero provision visible rather than hidden in a single line.

7. Library Access by Disadvantage Level

Disadvantaged socioeconomic groups receive the lowest library provision per resident. Although all groups remain under-served overall, the least disadvantaged SA2s still average noticeably better access than Melbourne's most disadvantaged communities.

8. Access, Disadvantage and Car Dependency

The worst-served cell is the most disadvantaged and most car-dependent. SA2s in IRSD deciles 1-3 with high car ownership record virtually zero library provision - a double bind where public transport is absent and library infrastructure has never been built. Low-car areas in this same decile group, by contrast, include inner-city pockets that happen to have high provision, exposing how uneven the geographic distribution truly is.

The Stakes: Education and Long-Term Opportunity

Library access is not merely a convenience. In areas where schools are stretched and private tutoring is unaffordable, public libraries serve as a critical educational equaliser. The final charts examine whether their absence leaves a measurable mark on educational outcomes.

9. Education Outcomes and Library Access

SA2s with libraries consistently sit at the upper end of the education distribution in their decile. The teal circles mark areas that contain at least one public library - they cluster toward the top of each decile's box, while library-free SA2s pull the median down. In the most disadvantaged deciles, nearly all SA2s have no library and record the lowest education rates. This chart shows that library access and education outcomes are linked across every level of disadvantage.

10. Who Lives Without a Library?

Nearly all residents in disadvantaged deciles live in library-free SA2s. The teal sliver in lower deciles is vanishingly thin - over 85% of people in deciles 1-3 reside in a statistical area that contains no public library at all. Higher deciles show a more even split, reflecting both greater infrastructure investment and the smaller, denser SA2 boundaries in established suburbs.
